Privacy policy

Scanvy reads labels. This page explains what stays on your iPhone, what reaches our server, and who else sees it.

No. 01

In short

  • Your library, photos and lists stay on your iPhone. We don't have a copy.
  • Our server keeps an anonymous account so it can count your daily reads. We never ask for a name, email address or phone number.
  • Cloud reading is off until you turn it on. When it's on, only the framed part of a label and the text your phone already found go to our server and to Anthropic, the company that makes Claude.
  • Product photos send only a retail barcode number to our server, never a photo, and you can turn them off. Signing in with Apple or Google is optional and stores no name or email address.
  • No ads, no tracking, no third-party analytics. We don't sell data.
  • You can delete your account and its server data from the app at any time.

No. 02

On your iPhone

The photos you take, the numbers read from them, and your notes, tags and collections are stored in Scanvy's own storage on the iPhone. They leave the phone only when you send them yourself, for example by exporting a CSV file and sharing it.

Scanvy uses the camera to photograph labels. If you import pictures, it gets only the ones you pick in the photo picker and copies them into its own storage. Deleting the app removes the library from that iPhone.

No. 03

Your anonymous account

The first time Scanvy opens, it uses Apple's App Attest to show our server that it is a genuine copy of the app on a real device. The server then opens an account with no name, email address or phone number attached. For that account it stores:

  • the App Attest key identifier and public key of your device,
  • a sign-in token, saved only as a one-way hash,
  • your time zone, read once when the account is opened, so the free daily reads reset at your local midnight,
  • how many reads you have used, and a short record of each read (when it happened, whether it finished, whether the cloud was used).

The account stays until you delete it. The read records are deleted after 30 days.

No. 04

Cloud reading

Most labels are read on the iPhone itself, with Apple's built-in text and barcode recognition. Cloud reading is a second reading for labels the phone isn't sure about. It stays off until you choose "Use cloud reading" during setup or turn on "Second reading in the cloud" under More. You can turn it off there at any time.

When it's on and a label needs it, Scanvy sends:

  • the framed area of the photo around the label, not the whole picture,
  • the text lines and barcode contents the phone already found in that area,
  • your language and region setting, so the answer comes back in the right form.

Our server passes this to Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, which reads the characters and returns the text. Anthropic works for us as a processor and handles the data only to give us that answer. Under Anthropic's commercial terms, API inputs and outputs are deleted within 30 days by default and are not used to train models. Content flagged for breaking Anthropic's usage policy may be kept longer. Anthropic explains this at privacy.claude.com.

On our side, the image is deleted as soon as the read ends, at the latest after 2 hours. The result and the text lines sent with it are kept for 7 days, so the app can still collect them if it was closed in the meantime, and are then removed.

Labels sometimes carry personal details, such as a name on a shipping label. If you don't want those read, leave them out of the frame.

No. 05

Product photos

For items that carry a retail barcode, Scanvy can add a photo of the product to your library. This is on by default. You can turn it off under More with the "Product photos" switch.

When an item has a retail barcode (EAN, UPC or GTIN), the app sends only that barcode number to our server, together with your anonymous account's sign-in token. Your photos and serial numbers are never sent for this.

Our server then asks Open Food Facts, Open Beauty Facts, Open Pet Food Facts and Open Products Facts for a photo of that product. These are open product databases run by the non-profit Open Food Facts. The request comes from our server, so those sites never see your IP address or your account. Our server keeps a list of barcode numbers and the address of the photo found, with no account attached, for up to 30 days, or 7 days when no photo was found.

The photo comes back to the iPhone and is saved with the item as a PNG file. These photos are shared by their contributors under the CC BY-SA licence, and the app shows that credit under the photo.

No. 06

Optional sign-in

Signing in is optional. Under More, Account, you can sign in with Apple or with Google so your reads and Scanvy Pro work on your other devices too.

Scanvy asks Apple for no name and no email address, and asks Google only for the basic "openid" sign-in. Our server checks the sign-in token with Apple or Google and keeps only which of the two you used and the user identifier that service gives Scanvy, linked to your anonymous account. It keeps no name and no email address.

Signing out on a device unlinks that device. Signing out on the first device you signed in on removes the identifier and unlinks your other devices as well. Deleting the account also removes the identifier.

No. 07

Countries, regions and your IP address

Claude is offered only in the countries and regions Anthropic supports (Anthropic's list). For that reason Scanvy isn't sold in mainland China, Hong Kong, Macau or Russia. If a cloud read comes from a place outside Anthropic's list, our server turns it down before anything is stored, and the phone reads the label on its own.

To tell where a request comes from, the server looks up your IP address in a country database kept on the server itself (DB-IP Lite). The lookup happens in memory and no outside service is asked. The IP address isn't saved with your account. Like any web server, ours writes short-lived request logs that include IP addresses, which we use for security and to fix problems.

No. 08

Purchases

Scanvy Pro is sold through the App Store. Apple handles the payment, so we never see your card, your Apple Account or your name. When you buy or restore, the app sends the signed transaction Apple gave it, and our server checks it with Apple before Pro turns on. Apple also tells our server about renewals, cancellations and refunds.

We keep the transaction details (product, dates and Apple's transaction identifiers) linked to your account until you delete the account.

No. 09

What we don't do

  • No advertising and no advertising identifier.
  • No tracking across other apps or websites.
  • No third-party analytics or advertising SDKs in the app.
  • We don't sell your data or share it for marketing.
  • This website sets no cookies and loads nothing from other sites.

No. 11

Deleting your data

In the app, open More and tap "Delete my account and server data". Our server then removes your account, sign-in tokens, the Apple or Google identifier if you signed in, read records, cloud results, any images still waiting, and the links between your account and your purchases. What remains are anonymous daily totals, such as how many reads happened on a given day, with no account attached. Nightly backups of the server database are kept for 7 days, so deleted data is gone from them within a week.

Deleting the account doesn't cancel a subscription, which belongs to your Apple Account. To cancel, open the Settings app, tap your name, then Subscriptions. Your library on the iPhone stays until you delete it or remove the app.
